SEO Interview Preparation: 70 Most Important Questions and Answers
Short, simple answers for interview revision. Learn the idea and example, then explain it in your own words. The questions cover SEO fundamentals, on-page SEO, technical SEO, off-page SEO, local SEO, tools, analytics, and practical scenarios.
1. What is SEO?
Answer: SEO (Search Engine Optimization) improves a website so it can appear more prominently in unpaid search results and attract relevant visitors.
2. Why is SEO important for a business?
Answer: It helps people find a business when they search for its products or services. It can bring relevant traffic, leads, trust, and sales over time.
3. What are the main types of SEO?
Answer: On-page SEO improves page content and elements; technical SEO improves crawling and indexing; off-page SEO builds reputation through external signals; local SEO improves visibility for location-based searches.
4. What is the difference between SEO and SEM/PPC?
Answer: SEO earns unpaid organic visibility and usually takes time. PPC uses paid ads that can appear quickly but require an advertising budget. SEM may mean paid search or search marketing broadly.
5. What is a SERP?
Answer: A Search Engine Results Page is the page shown after a search. It can include organic results, ads, maps, images, videos, and featured snippets.
6. What is organic traffic?
Answer: Visitors who reach a website by clicking unpaid search results. It is different from paid, direct, social, and referral traffic.
7. What is a keyword?
Answer: A keyword is a word or phrase people type into a search engine, such as “best laptops for students.” SEO uses keywords to understand what pages should answer.
8. What is search intent?
Answer: Search intent is the reason behind a search: informational (learn), navigational (find a site), commercial (compare options), or transactional (take action or buy).
9. What is keyword research?
Answer: It is finding and evaluating search terms by relevance, demand, competition, and intent, then choosing the best terms for suitable pages.
10. What is the difference between short-tail and long-tail keywords?
Answer: Short-tail keywords are broad, like “laptop.” Long-tail keywords are more specific, like “best laptop for coding under ₹60,000.” Long-tail terms can attract more targeted visitors.
11. How does Google Search work?
Answer: Google discovers pages through crawling, processes and may store them through indexing, then ranks relevant results for each query. Crawling does not guarantee indexing, and indexing does not guarantee high rankings.
12. What is crawling?
Answer: Crawling is when a search engine bot discovers and fetches webpages. Google’s crawler is commonly called Googlebot.
13. What is indexing?
Answer: Indexing is when Google processes a page and may store it in its search index so it can be considered for search results.
14. What is ranking?
Answer: Ranking is the position a page receives for a particular query. It depends on many signals, including relevance, content quality, usability, and search context.
15. What is robots.txt?
Answer: It gives compliant crawlers instructions about which URL paths they may crawl. It is not a security tool and does not reliably remove a URL from search results.
16. What is an XML sitemap?
Answer: It lists important URLs to help search engines discover them. Submitting a sitemap does not guarantee that every URL will be crawled or indexed.
17. What is a backlink?
Answer: A backlink is a link from another website to yours. Relevant, naturally earned links can support reputation and bring referral traffic.
18. What is a title tag?
Answer: The HTML title element names a page and may appear as the clickable title in search results. Make it unique, accurate, useful, and naturally relevant to the topic.
19. What is a meta description?
Answer: It summarizes a page and may appear below its title in search results. A clear description may encourage clicks, but Google can show different text; it is not a direct ranking factor.
20. What is CTR in SEO?
Answer: Click-through rate is the percentage of impressions that lead to clicks. Formula: clicks ÷ impressions × 100. For example, 50 clicks from 1,000 impressions is 5%.
21. What is an internal link?
Answer: A link from one page on your website to another page on the same website. Internal links help people navigate and help crawlers discover related pages.
22. What is duplicate content?
Answer: Substantially similar content available at multiple URLs. It is not automatically a penalty, but it can confuse URL selection and indexing. Canonicals, redirects, or URL improvements may help.
23. What is a canonical tag?
Answer: It signals which URL is preferred among duplicate or very similar pages. Google treats it as a signal, not an absolute command.
24. What is a 301 redirect?
Answer: A 301 tells browsers and search engines that a page has permanently moved. Redirect to a relevant replacement and avoid unnecessary redirect chains.
25. What is white-hat versus black-hat SEO?
Answer: White-hat SEO follows search guidelines and focuses on useful content and legitimate promotion. Black-hat SEO uses manipulative tactics such as link schemes, cloaking, and keyword stuffing.
26. What is on-page SEO?
Answer: It is optimizing individual pages, including content, titles, headings, URLs, images, and internal links, so users and search engines can understand them.
27. How do you optimize a webpage for SEO?
Answer: Understand the audience and intent, research keywords, improve title and headings, write useful content, create a readable URL, optimize images and internal links, check technical accessibility, then measure performance.
28. What is an H1 tag?
Answer: H1 is a main heading that communicates a page’s primary topic. Keep it clear and consistent with the page content.
29. What is the difference between H1, H2, and H3?
Answer: H1 identifies the main topic; H2s organize major sections; H3s organize subsections within those sections. Use headings in a logical hierarchy.
30. What is keyword density? Is there an ideal percentage?
Answer: Keyword density is how often a keyword appears compared with total words. There is no required ideal percentage; use natural language and cover the topic helpfully.
31. What is keyword stuffing?
Answer: It is unnaturally repeating keywords to manipulate rankings. It harms readability and may violate spam policies. Use terms naturally instead.
32. What is a URL slug?
Answer: It is the descriptive part of a URL, such as “best-running-shoes.” Keep it readable, relevant, and simple, and avoid changing established URLs without a plan.
33. What is keyword mapping?
Answer: It assigns target keywords and search intent to the most suitable pages. It helps prevent multiple pages from competing unnecessarily for the same need.
34. What is keyword cannibalization?
Answer: It is when pages target very similar search intent and compete for visibility. Compare their purpose and performance, then differentiate them or consolidate overlapping pages when appropriate.
35. What is content optimization?
Answer: Improving content so it is accurate, useful, easy to read, well structured, and matches search intent. Add helpful examples, update outdated facts, and remove repetition.
36. What is thin content?
Answer: Content that provides little original value or does not satisfy the user’s need. Improve it with useful information or combine/remove pages that serve no meaningful purpose. Short content is not automatically thin.
37. What is E-E-A-T?
Answer: Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness. These concepts appear in Google’s quality evaluation guidance; E-E-A-T is not a single direct ranking factor or score.
38. What is semantic SEO?
Answer: It focuses on the meaning and context of a topic, not just exact-match keywords. A useful running-shoes guide might cover fit, cushioning, support, and durability where relevant.
39. What is image SEO and alt text?
Answer: Image SEO helps images load efficiently and be understood. Alt text describes meaningful image content for screen-reader users and can help search engines; decorative images usually use empty alt text.
40. What is anchor text?
Answer: The clickable words in a link. It should naturally and accurately describe the destination, such as “SEO beginner’s guide.”
41. What are Core Web Vitals?
Answer: They measure loading, responsiveness, and visual stability: LCP (good: ≤2.5 seconds), INP (≤200 ms), and CLS (≤0.1), assessed at the 75th percentile. Good scores do not guarantee rankings.
42. What is mobile-first indexing?
Answer: Google primarily uses the mobile version of a website’s content for indexing. Ensure important content, metadata, images, and links are available on mobile.
43. What is structured data?
Answer: A standardized format, often JSON-LD using Schema.org vocabulary, that helps search engines understand page information. It may make a page eligible for enhanced results but does not guarantee them.
44. Canonical tag vs 301 redirect: what is the difference?
Answer: A canonical tag signals the preferred URL while both pages remain accessible. A 301 sends visitors and crawlers to a new URL and is suitable when a page has permanently moved.
45. What is technical SEO?
Answer: It improves a site’s technical setup so search engines can discover, crawl, render, and index pages. It includes sitemaps, robots.txt, redirects, canonicals, HTTPS, speed, and JavaScript rendering.
46. Crawlability vs indexability: what is the difference?
Answer: Crawlability means a search engine can access and fetch a page. Indexability means the page is eligible to be included in the search index.
47. Robots.txt vs noindex: what is the difference?
Answer: Robots.txt controls crawler access to URL paths. A noindex directive asks search engines not to index a page when they can crawl and see the directive. Blocking a page may prevent Google from seeing noindex.
48. How do you check whether a page is indexed?
Answer: Use Google Search Console’s URL Inspection tool and review the indexing status and reported issues. A site: search can be a rough check but is not definitive.
49. What are common HTTP status codes in SEO?
Answer: 200 means success; 301 permanent redirect; 302 temporary redirect; 404 not found; 410 gone; 403 forbidden; 5xx server error. Choose the response that accurately reflects the page’s situation.
50. 301 vs 302 redirect: what is the difference?
Answer: 301 is for a permanent move; 302 is for a temporary move. Use the code that matches the actual situation.
51. How would you fix a 404 error?
Answer: Check whether the page should exist. Update broken internal links, redirect a moved page to a relevant replacement, restore a page that should exist, or keep a proper 404/410 if it is gone with no suitable replacement.
52. What is a redirect chain or loop?
Answer: A chain sends a URL through several redirects before the final page; point directly to the final destination where possible. A loop redirects URLs repeatedly without reaching a page; fix conflicting redirect rules.
53. Why is HTTPS important for SEO?
Answer: HTTPS encrypts data between the browser and website, supports security and trust, and is a lightweight Google ranking signal. Ensure certificates and redirects are configured correctly.
54. What are LCP, INP, and CLS?
Answer: LCP measures loading of the largest visible content; INP measures responsiveness to interactions; CLS measures unexpected layout movement. Improve them by optimizing key content, reducing blocking JavaScript, and reserving space for images or ads.
55. How would you improve a slow website?
Answer: Measure first with PageSpeed Insights or Lighthouse. Then address the biggest issues, such as oversized images, slow server response, render-blocking resources, excessive JavaScript, caching, or layout shifts, and retest.
56. What is JavaScript SEO?
Answer: It ensures search engines can access and understand content and links rendered with JavaScript. Check rendered content, crawlable links, accessible resources, metadata, canonical tags, and rendering errors.
57. A page is not indexed. What would you do?
Answer: Inspect the URL in Search Console. Check status code, robots.txt, noindex, canonical, sitemap, internal links, content usefulness, and crawlability. Fix the cause and request indexing if appropriate, then monitor it.
58. Organic traffic suddenly drops. How would you investigate?
Answer: Confirm the drop in Search Console and Analytics, compare equivalent periods, and identify affected pages and queries. Check indexing, technical changes, demand, competitors, search-result changes, and manual actions or updates before deciding on fixes.
59. How would you perform a technical SEO audit?
Answer: Crawl the site and review Search Console. Check indexability, status codes, redirects, canonicals, sitemap, robots.txt, internal links, HTTPS, structured data, mobile rendering, and Core Web Vitals. Prioritize by impact and effort.
60. What is off-page SEO?
Answer: Activities outside the website that can improve reputation and visibility, such as earning relevant backlinks, digital PR, brand mentions, and promoting useful content.
61. What makes a good backlink?
Answer: It comes naturally from a relevant, trustworthy site, appears in useful context, and helps readers. Relevance and quality matter more than simply counting links.
62. How do you build backlinks safely?
Answer: Create useful, link-worthy resources; do relevant outreach; build publisher relationships; use digital PR; and avoid link farms or paid link schemes that manipulate rankings.
63. What is local SEO?
Answer: It improves visibility for searches tied to a place, such as “bakery near me.” Key work includes accurate business details, a well-maintained Google Business Profile, relevant local pages, reviews, and consistent contact information.
64. What is Google Business Profile?
Answer: A free Google business listing that can show a business in Search and Maps. Keep its name, address or service area, hours, category, contact details, and services accurate.
65. What is Google Search Console?
Answer: A tool that reports how a website performs in Google Search and helps diagnose indexing issues. It shows clicks, impressions, CTR, average position, indexing information, and other search reports.
66. What is Google Analytics 4 (GA4)?
Answer: An analytics tool for measuring website or app activity, including sessions, engagement, and conversions. Search Console focuses on Google Search visibility; GA4 helps analyze what users do after arriving.
67. Which SEO metrics do you track?
Answer: I track organic clicks, impressions, CTR, average position, indexed important pages, qualified organic sessions, leads, conversions, and revenue where available. The best metrics depend on the business goal.
68. How do you prioritize SEO tasks?
Answer: I rank tasks by business impact, number and importance of pages affected, confidence in the diagnosis, and effort required. I fix serious indexing or sitewide problems before low-impact cosmetic changes.
69. How would you improve a new website’s SEO?
Answer: Set up Search Console and analytics, make the site crawlable and indexable, create a sitemap, plan useful pages around search intent, optimize titles and content, build internal links, ensure mobile usability and speed, and monitor results.
70. Why should we hire you for an SEO role?
Answer: “I understand SEO fundamentals, keyword research, on-page optimization, technical SEO, and performance analysis. I use data to identify problems, prioritize improvements by business impact, and focus on useful content and user experience. I’m ready to keep learning as search evolves.” Adjust this to match your real skills and experience.
